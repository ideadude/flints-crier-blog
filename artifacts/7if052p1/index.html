Research Brief

Company: Strong

Best-in-class cruise operator. Superior margins (50.9% gross, 24.4% profit vs peers at 43-56% gross, 6-11% profit). Brand strength, scale advantages, operational excellence. Revenue growing 11.3%.

Management: Strong

Consistent earnings beats (last 8 quarters), raised FY guidance, tripled dividend (from $0.55 to $1.50/quarter signals confidence). Investing in AI for pricing/booking optimization. Capital allocation improving.

Valuation: Fair/Full

P/E 15.9 (right at Jason's ~15 benchmark), but P/S 3.8 (above ~2x benchmark). Premium reflects quality execution vs peers (CCL P/E 10.9, NCLH 12.5). Analyst consensus $340 (+30.8% upside) but limited margin of safety at current price.

Trend: Pullback in long-term uptrend

Price testing 200 DMA ($260) after lower highs since June 2025 peak ($366). Below 50 DMA ($268). Medium-term bearish, long-term structure still intact IF this support holds.

In Our Lane: Medium conviction

Consumer discretionary, not tech. Cruise industry requires understanding competitive dynamics, consumer spending patterns, and capital intensity. Jason's edge is patience and holding through volatility, but not deep domain expertise here.

3-5yr Thesis

Travel demand remains resilient post-COVID. RCL continues outexecuting peers on margins and guest experience. AI investment improves pricing power and operational efficiency. Dividend growth attracts income investors. Stock compounds at mid-teens annually from current levels as earnings grow and multiple holds or expands modestly. Downside protected by strong balance sheet recovery and market-leading position.

Catalysts

  • Next earnings (July 28, 2026) — guidance raise would confirm strength
  • Continued dividend growth — signals cash flow confidence
  • New ship deliveries and capacity growth
  • AI-driven margin expansion over next 12-18 months

Main Risk

Consumer discretionary slowdown. Recession or sustained travel pullback crushes bookings and pricing power. High leverage (217% D/E) amplifies downside. If consumers tighten, cruises are easy to cut. Recent selloff may be market sniffing out demand weakness.

Bottom Line

Royal Caribbean is the best operator in the cruise industry — superior margins, strong execution, improving capital allocation. The business is firing on all cylinders (beating earnings, raising guidance, tripling dividend). But the stock is testing critical support at the 200 DMA after 6 months of lower highs.

Valuation is fair but not screaming cheap (P/E at benchmark, P/S elevated). The premium reflects quality, but there's limited margin of safety. The technical setup is a knife edge: hold $260 and it's still a long-term bull in a medium-term chop. Break below and next stop is $245 or lower.

For Jason's 3-5 year timeline: this is a watchlist name, not a slam-dunk buy today. If it holds $260 and bounces, or if it drops to $245 and finds support, it becomes more interesting. The thesis is solid, but consumer discretionary in a potential slowdown warrants caution. Better to accumulate on weakness than chase strength here.
