Tap Notes: Two Hundred Bugfixes Later

November 19, 2026

Generating code is now the cheap part. Today’s reading keeps landing on what comes after: the review queue, the maintenance backlog, and the quiet gates that decide whether your setup does what you think it does. The numbers are starting to show up.

The review tax, quantified

Harvard researchers find AI coding agents shifted the bottleneck to code review A Harvard working paper covering 725,938 workers at 718 firms found that engineers at agent-adopting companies wrote about 30% more code. Finished work didn’t measurably increase, review took 49% longer, and change requests nearly doubled.

Why it matters: This is the “agent writes it, human owns it” tax with real numbers attached. The useful part is the two-channel framing. If the problem is volume, more drafts hitting the same reviewers, you fix it by moving labor into review. If it’s quality, drafts bouncing back, you need a better model. Any vendor selling you an agent should be able to say which channel their product lives in. Ask for time-to-approved-work-product, send-back rate, and reviewer hours. Another detail: roughly 80% of firms had AI review tools, and AI comments made up only 9% of review comments. Auto-review didn’t clear the queue. Caveats: it’s a job-market paper without completed peer review, and it was produced with a data vendor that has a commercial stake. The article says so plainly, which is why I trust it.

The clone doesn’t ship the fixes

Quoting Daniel Stenberg While agents converted 185,000 lines of curl’s C into 340,000 lines of Rust, the real curl project merged over 300 commits.

We actually improved curl during these a little more than three weeks. A little over 200 bugfixes actually.
Post to X

Why it matters: A conformance harness can tell you the port passes today’s tests. It can’t tell you the port will keep absorbing the next 200 fixes. A codebase is a living thing, and a rewrite is a snapshot. Stenberg’s closing question, “will these clones keep up?”, is the maintenance bill nobody puts in the demo. Read the full post.

Footguns in the toolchain

Claude Code reads AGENTS.md only when telemetry is on (from September 2026) The AGENTS.md loader sat behind a remote feature flag that failed closed when telemetry-related environment variables were set. A privacy setting silently turned off an unrelated local file read. The author’s workaround was a one-line CLAUDE.md that imports AGENTS.md.

Why it matters: The bug is minor, and the design is the lesson. A local feature gated on a remote flag should fail to its documented behavior, not to off, and it should say something when it does. Also worth stealing: the author’s canary-word method for proving whether an instruction file was actually read. Run it on your own setup. Don’t assume it works.

How Cloudflare WAF helps protect against Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938) A critical memory-corruption flaw in OpenJDK’s Base64 decoder can lead to remote code execution. Cloudflare shipped six managed WAF rules for paid plans, logging matches by default.

Why it matters: The WAF rules are vendor marketing. The vulnerability is not. A bug in a decoder that nearly everything Java-shaped links against means a long list of affected products. Patch the runtimes. Treat the WAF as a tripwire while you do, not a substitute.

The cheap-executor question

Why isn’t the industry freaking out about DeepSeek 4.1 Flash? (from October 2026) After a month of heavy use, the author argued the model matched frontier models like Claude Opus on most coding and planning tasks at a fraction of the cost. They credit a roughly 437x KV cache reduction, which kept all-day sessions under $1.

Why it matters: Skip the “indistinguishable from Opus” claim, which needs harder evals. The mechanism is the story: when long context gets that cheap, “good enough” stops being a compromise and becomes a routing strategy. Cheap executor, expensive reviewer. That pairs with Harvard’s point above. If review is the bottleneck, spend your best model there and let the cheap one draft.

Cheap drafts, expensive verification. The curl team is still shipping fixes while the clones sit still. 🪨
