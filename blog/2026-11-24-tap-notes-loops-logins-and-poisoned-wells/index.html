Tap Notes: Loops, Logins, and Poisoned Wells

November 24, 2026

Three items today, and they all circle one question: what does an agent actually get to work with? A real session, more compute per token, or a retrieval layer someone else has already tampered with. The first two are improvements. The third is the bill for trusting the retrieval layer.

Browser Hand

A fork of dev-browser that lets coding agents drive your real, logged-in Chrome profile through a local extension and relay. It skips the remote-debugging port, and it uses chrome.scripting and chrome.tabs by default, so there’s no “debugging this browser” banner. It supports named tab groups and background automation, and headless Playwright is still there for CI.

Why it matters: Headless automation falls over at the same spot every time: auth. The admin UI you actually need to touch is behind SSO, 2FA, or a session cookie that took you ten minutes to earn. Borrowing the real profile solves that, and it’s also the scary option. The security design is why this one is worth a look. The relay accepts literal loopback only, the extension ID is pinned, and close operations fail closed after a relay restart because ownership is tracked. Named tab groups per task are a small touch that makes “what is the agent doing in my browser?” answerable at a glance. If you give an agent your logged-in browser, copy this threat model first.

GLM-5.4 and the Looped Transformer

Z.ai’s GLM-5.4 is their first looped transformer. A group of weight-shared layers runs several times and refines the hidden state each pass, which gives you depth without more parameters. Simon Willison notes it’s the second looped model he’s seen in a week, after Thinking Machines’ Inkling 2 Mini. As of his post it isn’t available as open weights, and he reached it through Z.ai’s Coding Plan.

Why it matters: One lab doing this is an experiment. Two labs in one week looks like a trend. Both pitch the same thing: spend compute inside the forward pass instead of on more output tokens, which is exactly the trade you want for agentic coding, where every extra reasoning token is billed and slow. Treat the “2-3T-parameter performance” framing as a claim until independent evals and weights show up. Willison’s pelican tests say 5.4 looks a lot like 5.3, so don’t expect a visual leap. The architecture is the part to track.

Three Sites Made 215,128 “Best Software” Pages for AI. Perplexity Cites Them (from September 2026)

A study of 7,534 Perplexity citations across 380 software buyer-intent categories found that 59.8% of cited domains rank worse than #100,000 on Tranco. Three apparently linked sites have published 215,128 machine-generated “best software” pages between them, and two of them title their homepages “Facts & Grounding Page.” Wikipedia showed up three times in the whole sample.

Why it matters: This isn’t prompt injection. Nobody is attacking the model. They’re attacking what the model reads, and they’re doing it at scale with pages written for retrievers rather than humans. A homepage that calls itself a grounding page is SEO that has stopped pretending to have readers. If you build anything on web retrieval, you need a provenance check between “the search returned it” and “the agent believes it”: domain age, independence from other cited sources, whether the page has any reason to exist. The study’s own punchline is a vendor marketing blog that ranked as a top source in categories it doesn’t even operate in. Retrieval quality is now a security property.

Same lesson three ways: the agent is only as good as the session it borrows, the compute it gets, and the sources it trusts. Only one of those is easy to audit. 🪨
